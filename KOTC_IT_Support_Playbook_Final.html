KOTC Support Operating Principles

The following principles apply to every support activity performed under the KOTC Outsourcing Project. They are mandatory for all Helpdesk, End User Support, and Asset Team staff, and they apply regardless of who requests the work or how the request is received.

Operating PrincipleOperational Expectation
Helpdesk is the Single Point of Contact (SPOC)All user contact for IT support is received, logged, and tracked through the Helpdesk, whatever channel the user uses. Requests received directly by an engineer must be routed back to the Helpdesk for logging.
No support activity without a ticketNo work is started, performed, or completed unless a ticket exists in BMC Helix or an approved contingency record has been raised.
No dark ticketsWork carried out informally, verbally, or outside BMC Helix is not permitted. Every action taken must be traceable to a ticket.
All activities must be recorded in HelixDiagnosis, actions taken, user contact, asset movement, and resolution are recorded in the ticket as they happen, not reconstructed afterwards.
User communication is mandatoryUsers are informed at logging, on progress, on delay, and at resolution. An open ticket with no user update is a service quality failure.
Escalate before SLA breachTickets at risk are escalated to the correct resolver group or to the Helpdesk Supervisor before the target is missed, not after.
Maintain audit-ready documentation at all timesEvery ticket, form, register, and report must be complete enough to stand as evidence in an internal or external audit without rework.

Process Overview

The ICT Service Desk is the first point of contact for KOTC users. Users may contact Helpdesk by phone, email, Easy Memo, walk-in, or emergency mobile for approved critical cases.

Every support activity must be recorded in BMC Helix. This includes incidents, service requests, work orders, changes, problems, security incidents, and major incidents.

The Helpdesk provides first-level support. This means the Helpdesk receives the request, verifies the user, checks for existing tickets, logs the ticket, performs basic troubleshooting, updates the user, and escalates the ticket when required.

If the issue is simple and within Helpdesk scope, the Helpdesk should try to resolve it at first contact. This is counted as First Call Resolution, also called FCR.

If the issue cannot be resolved by Helpdesk, it must be escalated to the correct support group with complete description, troubleshooting notes in detail, screenshots, and user information.

Desktop / Field Engineers handle onsite support, hardware support, asset replacement, printer/copier issues, vessel visits, and other technical activities assigned to them.

Specialist teams, backend teams, or vendors handle issues that require advanced access, system-level changes, application support, network/server support, or vendor action.

The Helpdesk remains responsible for tracking, follow-up, user updates, and SLA monitoring until the ticket is resolved, fulfilled, cancelled, or closed.

Important rule:
No official support activity should be handled only verbally. If work is done, it must be recorded in BMC Helix or in the approved contingency form when BMC Helix is unavailable.

BMC Helix Ticket Quality Standards

BMC Helix is the official ITSM tool for recording, tracking, assigning, updating, resolving, fulfilling, closing, and reporting IT support activities. All tickets must be clear, complete, factual, and audit ready.

Ticket title, category, description, assignment, work notes, resolution, and closure details must match. Tickets with incomplete or incorrect information affect SLA tracking, reporting accuracy, audit compliance, FCR calculation, and service quality.

Ticket Quality Rules

Ticket AreaStandard Rule
TitleMust be short, clear, and meaningful. Use 3–4 words where possible. Example: “Outlook not opening”, “Printer toner replacement”, “Laptop not powering”.
DescriptionMust include what happened, user impact, location, affected asset/service, error message, and required action.
CategoryMust match the actual issue or request. Select the correct Category, Subcategory, and Item / Tier 3 classification.
PriorityMust be based on impact and urgency, not user pressure only. Any priority change must be justified in ticket notes.
Work NotesMust show troubleshooting performed, user contact, follow-up actions, escalation details, and important updates.
Pending StatusMust include the correct pending reason, such as user response, vendor action, vessel ETA, approval, or external dependency.
AssignmentTickets must be assigned to the correct resolver group or fulfillment team with complete information.
ResolutionMust clearly explain what was done to resolve the issue or fulfill the request. Do not use only “done”, “completed”, or “fixed”.
ClosureClosure notes must be complete, factual, and audit ready. User confirmation should be recorded where applicable.

Minimum Ticket Documentation Requirements

  • Each ticket must include:
  • User name and contact details
  • Location / vessel name, where applicable
  • Issue or request summary
  • Affected service, system, application, printer, device, or CI where applicable
  • Correct ticket type: Incident, Service Request, Work Order, Change, Problem, Security Incident, or Major Incident
  • Correct category and priority
  • Troubleshooting or fulfillment steps
  • Attachments, screenshots, or logs where available
  • Escalation or reassignment details where applicable
  • Pending reason and follow-up notes where applicable
  • Clear resolution or fulfillment notes before closure

Tickets must not be left idle without update. If the ticket cannot be resolved by Helpdesk, it must be escalated with full notes, and the user must be informed.

Incident Management Guide

17.1 What is an Incident?

An incident is any unplanned interruption, reduction in quality, or failure of an IT service impacting normal business operations.

Examples:

System or application not working.

Email issues

Network connectivity problems.

Virus or malware alerts

Hardware failure

Security incidents affecting users.

Priorities will be assigned to the incidents based on the service levels as determined and agreed by KOTC IT.

Priority is calculated based on URGENCY and IMPACT. URGENCY/IMPACT can be assigned to a particular subject or to a user to allot a relevant PRIORITY to that Incident.

17.5 FCR Rules

FCR means First Call Resolution.

If Helpdesk resolves the incident without reassignment, mark FCR as Yes.

If the ticket is escalated or reassigned, mark FCR as No and record the reason.

Monthly FCR target is minimum 30%.

If resolved by Helpdesk → FCR = Yes

If escalated → FCR = No (reason mandatory)

17.6 Escalation Rules

Escalate when:

Issue is beyond Helpdesk scope.

Technical access is required.

SLA breach risk exists.

Multiple users are affected.

Security concern exists.

Major Incident may be required.

Vendor or backend support is needed.

Before escalation, document troubleshooting performed, attach evidence, assign the correct resolver group, and inform the user.

Major Incident Guide

18.1 What is a Major Incident?

A Major Incident is a high-impact incident that causes major business disruption, affects critical services, affects multiple users, or requires urgent management coordination.

Major Incidents are always treated as Priority 1.

Examples:

ERP system unavailable

BMC Helix unavailable

Attendance system failure affecting multiple users.

Network outage affecting multiple departments.

Critical email or server outage

Management-declared major incident

18.2 Major Incident Criteria

An incident may be treated as a Major Incident if one or more apply:

Business-critical service is down.

Multiple users are affected.

High operational or business impact exists.

No workaround is available.

VIP / critical operation is impacted.

Management declares it as Major Incident

Service Request and Work Order Guide

20.1 What is a Service Request?

A Service Request is a standard user request for information, access, installation, configuration, movement, consumables, or approved operational support.

A Work Order is the BMC Helix record used to manage and fulfill a Service Request or assigned task.

An Incident is for a fault or service disruption. A Service Request is for a standard request or approved activity.

Examples:

Software installation or removal

Hardware installation or movement

Asset relocation

Access request

Email / distribution list request.

Mobile email configuration

Printer toner / cartridge replacement

Consumables request.

Vessel support request

Preventive maintenance request

Meeting room support request

20.2 Service Request Logging Rules

All Service Requests shall be logged in BMC Helix as per the approved Service Request Management Procedure.

  • The Helpdesk shall verify and record:
  • Username and contact details
  • Location / vessel name, where applicable
  • Request summary
  • Full request description
  • Required service or item
  • Affected asset, printer, device, application, or CI where applicable
  • Required approval, if applicable
  • Correct category and priority
  • Assigned fulfillment team or resolver group
  • Attachments, screenshots, memos, or approval evidence where available

20.3 Approval Requirement

Requests requiring management, security, application owner, asset owner, or business approval shall not proceed until the required approval is obtained and documented in BMC Helix.

If approval is rejected, the request shall be cancelled with clear notes, and the requester shall be informed.

20.5 Service Request Fulfillment Rules

  • Service Requests must be assigned to the correct fulfillment team.
  • The assigned team remains responsible for updates until the request is fulfilled, reassigned, cancelled, or closed.
  • Pending status must always include a valid pending reason.
  • Requests must not remain idle without update.
  • User communication must be recorded in ticket notes.
  • Fulfillment notes must clearly explain what was completed.
  • Do not close a Work Order with only “done”, “completed”, or “fixed”.
  • If the request requires a controlled change, RFC guidance must be followed before implementation.
  • If the request is incorrectly logged, it shall be corrected, cancelled, or re-logged under the correct process with proper remarks.

Important Rule

Service Requests are used for standard approved fulfillment. Incidents are used for service interruption or degradation. Change Requests are used when the activity modifies a controlled production service, system, configuration, or environment.

Problem Management Guide

Problem Management is used to find the root cause of recurring incidents, major incidents, unknown errors, or issues needing permanent corrective action.

A Problem record may be required when:

Same issue repeats for a user, location, system, application, printer, vessel, or group

Root cause is unknown.

Major Incident occurred and RCA is required.

Temporary workaround exists but permanent fix is still needed.

Same workaround is being used repeatedly.

Issue may cause SLA breaches or service disruption.

Supervisor, PM / SDM, management, or resolver team requests investigation

21.1 Helpdesk responsibility: • Identify repeated issues during ticket review or user calls. • Search previous tickets in BMC Helix before logging new tickets. • Inform the Helpdesk Supervisor or responsible resolver group when recurrence is noticed. • Link related incidents to the Problem record where applicable. • Use approved workarounds from Known Error or Knowledge Base records. • Update users based on the Incident ticket, not directly from the Problem record unless instructed.

21.2 Resolver team responsibility: • Investigate root cause. • Document workaround, root cause, permanent fix, and preventive action. • Create or update Known Error / Knowledge Base article where applicable. • Coordinate Change Request if permanent fix requires a controlled change.

Change Requests Guide

A Change Request, also called RFC (Request for Change), is required when an activity may modify a production service, system, application, infrastructure, configuration, access model, endpoint standard, network setting, documentation, or service component.

Unauthorized changes are not allowed. Support staff shall not perform changes without the required approval.

When is a Change Request Required?

A Change Request may be required for:

  • Server or application configuration changes.
  • Network, firewall, switch, router, Wi-Fi, or communication changes.
  • Mass software deployment or removal.
  • Major patch deployment.
  • Endpoint image or standard build changes.
  • Security configuration changes.
  • Production application upgrades or modifications.
  • Service Desk workflow, category, SLA, or notification changes.
  • CMDB / asset record changes that affect service control.
  • Any activity that may cause downtime, user impact, security impact, or business risk.

22.4 Minimum RFC Information

The following information must be included in every Change Request:

  • Change title and description.
  • Business reason / justification.
  • Affected service, system, location, users, or CI.
  • Impact and risk assessment.
  • Implementation plan.
  • Test / validation plan.
  • Rollback / backout plan.
  • Required approvals.

  • Communication requirement.
  • Post-implementation result.

22.5 Helpdesk / Engineer Responsibilities

  • Do not perform unauthorized changes.
  • Identify tickets that may require an RFC.
  • Inform the Helpdesk Supervisor or Change Coordinator before proceeding.
  • Link the Incident, Service Request, Problem, or Work Order to the Change Request where applicable.
  • Communicate to the user that the request requires approval or Change Management review.
  • Document all actions, approvals, and implementation details in BMC Helix.

Important Rule

A Service Request is used for standard approved fulfillment activities. A Change Request (RFC) is required whenever an activity modifies a controlled production service, system, configuration, infrastructure component, or environment.

Definitions

Incident — An unplanned interruption to an IT service or reduction in the quality of an IT service.

Service Request — A standard user request for information, access, installation, configuration, movement, consumables, or approved operational support.

Work Order — A BMC Helix record used to manage and fulfill a Service Request or assigned task.

Major Incident — A high-impact incident causing significant business disruption, multiple-user impact, critical service outage, or management-declared urgent incident requiring coordinated response.

Security Incident — An event that may affect confidentiality, integrity, or availability of IT systems or information, such as phishing, malware, unauthorized access, or data breach.

Problem — The cause or suspected cause of one or more incidents.

Known Error — A problem with a documented root cause and workaround or permanent fix.

Change / RFC — A formal request to add, modify, remove, or control a service, system, configuration, application, infrastructure component, or documentation.

Standard Change — A pre-approved, low-risk, repeatable change with defined steps.

Normal Change — A planned change requiring assessment, approval, scheduling, implementation, and closure.

Emergency Change — An urgent change required to restore critical service, prevent major business impact, or address urgent security risk.

SLA — Service Level Agreement defining agreed response and resolution targets.

FCR — First Call Resolution. This applies when Helpdesk resolves the issue during the initial contact without reassignment.

CI — Configuration Item, such as an asset, system, application, service, or component that is recorded and managed.

CMDB — Configuration Management Database used to maintain configuration and asset-related information.

KB — Knowledge Base containing approved known issues, workarounds, troubleshooting steps, and how-to documents.

RCA — Root Cause Analysis used to identify the underlying cause of a major or recurring issue.

PIR — Post Incident Review or Post Implementation Review used to review major incidents or significant changes after completion.

Pending — Ticket status used when action is waiting for user response, vendor action, vessel ETA, approval, or external dependency.

Resolver Group — The support group responsible for investigation, fulfillment, or resolution.

Escalation — Moving or raising a ticket to the correct technical team, management level, vendor, or specialist group based on impact, urgency, SLA risk, or scope.
